# Course builder — how to guide

A walkthrough of building, checking and sharing a post-16 programme. Allow about
ten minutes for your first builder.

For what the app is and how it is put together, see
[the overview](./course-builder-overview.md).

---

## Before you start

You need an account on a Pearson email domain (`@pearson.com`,
`@pearsoncanada.com` or `@pearsoned.com`). Sign in, then open **Course builder**
from the dashboard, or go straight to `/course`.

If you only want to look one qualification up, you do not need a builder at all —
use **Browse the guide** (`/course/guide`).

---

## 1. Create a course builder

On the hub, fill in **Create a course builder**:

1. **Name** it for the decision you are making, not the tool — for example
   *Level 3 Business options, September 2027*. The name becomes the public link,
   so it is worth getting roughly right now.
2. Pick a **subject** and **level** if you know them. These seed the search
   filters so the first screen already shows what you care about, and they unlock
   the option to keep visitors to that scope later.
3. **Create course builder.**

Anything here can be changed afterwards. Every builder starts as a draft: only
you, and people you share it with, can see it.

---

## 2. Find the qualifications you are considering

The left half of the workspace is the guide.

- **Search** by title or QN — *Extended Certificate*, or `601/7159/5`.
- **Filter** by subject, level, type (BTEC, T Level, A Level, V Level, NVQ and
  the certificates), and status for 2027.
- Tick **Funded for 2027 only** to hide anything without funding.
- The count under the filters tells you how many of the 287 qualifications match.
- **Clear filters** resets everything.

Each row is colour-coded: green is available and funded, amber is new or awaiting
funding confirmation, red is being withdrawn or not funded.

---

## 3. Read an entry before you commit to it

Click any row to open its full guide entry:

- Level, type, GLH, whether it is a full programme or part of one, the learner
  profile, and funding for 2027.
- What the guide asks you to do — keep teaching, plan a replacement, or nothing —
  and the year reform lands.
- The guide's **transition route**, in its own words.
- **Qualifications to consider**, each with *Find in guide* so you can look one up
  without losing your place.

**Add to plan** from inside the entry, or **Add** straight from the row.

---

## 4. Build the programme

The right half is your programme.

- Each choice shows its status, type, level and GLH.
- Add a short note against a choice — *why this sits in the programme* — for the
  colleague who reads this in six months.
- **✕** removes a choice.
- Use **Planning notes** at the bottom for staffing, timetabling, and what to
  confirm with your Curriculum Development Manager.

Set **First teach year** in the header to the year you are planning for. Every
check below is worded against that year.

Changes save themselves about a second after you stop typing — watch for *All
changes saved* next to the year. There is no save button.

---

## 5. Read the checks

**What to check** rewrites itself as you build. Read it top to bottom:

| Colour | Means | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | A real problem for your first teach year — not funded, or being withdrawn | Open the entry, read the transition route, swap the choice |
| Amber | Worth a look — funding unconfirmed, reform coming, an unusual shape, a stale choice | Confirm it before you commit |
| Green | Sound as it stands | Nothing to do |

Two checks are about shape rather than risk:

- **Built around a full programme** — one qualification carries the programme and
  the rest sit alongside it. If you see *more than one full programme*, a learner
  would normally take one of them, not several.
- **Comparable to / smaller than a full programme** — when nothing in the plan is
  a full programme, your total GLH is compared against the range that full
  programmes actually occupy at that level in the guide.

**The guide suggests considering** gathers the routes listed against your at-risk
choices. *Find in guide* searches for one so you can read it and add it.

---

## 6. Download it

**Download as Excel** gives you two sheets:

- **Programme** — one row per qualification, with QN, level, GLH, status,
  funding, reform year, the guide's transition wording, suggested routes, and
  your note.
- **Advice** — the summary and every check in full, so the file still makes sense
  to someone who was not in the room.

Visitors on a published builder also get **Print or save as PDF**.

---

## 7. Get it in front of other people

There are two routes, and they do different jobs.

### Share with named colleagues (private)

**Share**, enter a Pearson email, choose **Can view** or **Can edit**.

- *Can view* opens a read-only version of your programme and its checks.
- *Can edit* opens the full workspace — they browse, add, remove and note
  alongside you.
- Change or remove access any time from the same panel.
- Shared builders show up under **Shared with me** on their hub and dashboard.

Your planning notes stay private unless you tick **Show my notes on the shared
and embedded builder**.

### Publish a link (open to anyone)

**Publish** turns on `/cb/<your-builder>`. Anyone with the link gets a working
builder: the full guide, their own programme, their own download. Nothing they do
touches your copy, and their choices are never saved to Pearson — they stay in
that person's browser.

Then:

- **Copy link** to send it round.
- **View ↗** to see exactly what they see.
- **Embed** to copy an `<iframe>` snippet for a website or intranet page. A height
  of around 700px suits most pages; raise it if the page has no scrollbar of its
  own.
- **Unpublish** whenever you want; the link and any embed stop working
  immediately.

### Decide how the shared builder opens

Two tick-boxes in the header, under *On the shared and embedded builder*:

- **Start visitors off with the programme below** — they open holding your
  selection rather than an empty builder. Use it for a recommended starting
  point; leave it off if you want people to think it through themselves.
- **Keep visitors to this subject and level** — holds them to your scope, so a
  Level 3 Business builder shows Level 3 Business. Needs a subject or level set
  first.

---

## 8. Keep an eye on it

From the card on your hub or dashboard:

- **Analytics** — page views, unique visits, and what people searched, filtered
  and clicked, over 7, 30 or 90 days.
- **History** — who created, published, unpublished or duplicated it.
- **Duplicate** — copy it to work up a variant. A copy always starts as a draft.
- **Transfer** — hand ownership to another Pearson colleague, optionally keeping
  yourself as an editor.
- **Delete** — final. Shared colleagues lose access and any link or embed stops
  working. The confirmation tells you who is affected.

---

## If you are using someone else's builder

You do not need an account. Open the link and:

1. Search the guide and **Add** the qualifications you are considering.
2. Read **What to check** — it is about *your* choices, not the author's.
3. Add notes, then **Download as Excel** or **Print or save as PDF**.

Your work stays in your browser, so you can come back to the link and pick up
where you left off; **Start again** clears it. In a private window, or with site
data blocked, the builder still works — it just will not remember anything.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| *Could not save — check your connection* | The autosave failed. Stay on the page, fix the connection, then make one more edit to trigger a save. |
| *Course builder not available* on a link | It has been unpublished, or the link is wrong. Check it with whoever sent it. |
| *Not in the current edition of the guide* against a choice | A newer edition dropped it. Remove it and pick a current equivalent. |
| *GLH to be published* | The guide has not published hours for it yet, so it is excluded from your total — the panel says how many are affected. |
| *Guidance still to come* in an entry | The guide has no transition route for it yet. Speak to your Curriculum Development Manager. |
| Analytics or History says it is not set up | The database migration for that feature has not been applied to this environment. |
| A colleague cannot be added | Only Pearson email domains can be shared with. |
| Nothing matches your filters | Widen the subject or clear the status filter. |

---

## One thing to remember

Status, funding and reform years are the guide's position for **first teach
2027**. Use the builder to narrow the options and make the reasoning visible —
then confirm the programme with your Curriculum Development Manager before you
commit to it.
